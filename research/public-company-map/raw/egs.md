### 1.2 Google 396 MW PPA (announced 2026-09-01) — verified

This is **not** Cape Phase I first power. It is a **later Cape Station expansion**.

- **8-K filed 2026-09-01**, earliest event **2026-08-26**: Cape Generating Station 6 LLC entered a PPA with **Google Energy LLC** (Alphabet Inc. parent guaranty) for **396 MW EGS** at Cape Station, Beaver County, Utah. Delivery in **four successive 99 MW sub-tranches**. **Target commercial operation beginning in the third quarter of 2028**. **15-year delivery term**. Capacity-expansion option of **approximately 600 MW**, such that total contracted EGS capacity would be **not less than approximately 950 MW**, guaranteed COD of the expansion **no later than June 2030**, subject to Google’s acceptance and a definitive agreement. Project intended to use **Utah SB 132** flexible delivery pathways; **subject to regulatory approvals**. Expanded delivery capability targeted **first half of 2030**.
- **Company press release, 2026-09-01** (fervoenergy.com and GlobeNewswire): “396-megawatt (MW) power purchase agreement with Google… expected to come online in **2028**.” Option “to expand its offtake by approximately **600 MW**, for a total of nearly **1 GW**, by **June 2030**.” PPA **price not disclosed**.
- **Listing on the 8-K cover:** Class A Common Stock, **FRVO**, **The Nasdaq Stock Market LLC (NASDAQ Global Select Market)**. Listing as of this 8-K is verified.

## 8. Bear case, analog, kill criteria

### Analog (company-adjacent, not company statements)

EGS is not a 2020s invention.

- **Fenton Hill, New Mexico (Los Alamos), 1970s–1990s:** DOE HDR/EGS prototype. Demonstrated heat extraction from stimulated crystalline rock; **did not become a commercial IPP fleet**. This is the long-cycle analog: technical success ≠ cheap repeatable MWh.
- **Basel, Switzerland, 2006:** Deep Heat Mining stimulation induced seismicity (widely reported **M~3.4**, project halted). Public-acceptance analog.
- **Pohang, South Korea, 2017:** EGS-associated **M5.4** (scientific literature / government investigations; **not re-litigated here**). The analog is **induced seismicity as a permit- and offtake-killer**, not a well-cost issue.
- **Utah FORGE** sits next to Cape Station (Fervo site page). That is a **research** analog in the same county, not a commercial plant.

Fervo S-1 risk factors include operational disruptions, permitting, seismicity-adjacent “negative public perception and political opposition,” and interconnection. **Cape-specific induced-seismicity events: not in the record of the filings reviewed.**

### Bear case (tied to the record)

1. **Commissioning ≠ COD.** 8-K language is already “test power” then “full production.” Reservoir underperformance vs the assumed **83% CF** in the S-1 illustration, or well-count creep vs “8–10 wells per GeoBlock,” breaks the **$7,000 → $5,500 → $3,000/kW** path.
2. **Interconnection math.** S-1: Cape Phase II has **~290 MW** of interconnection/transmission rights vs **384 MW** expanded SCE+CPA contracted capacity. Google 396 MW is a **different** delivery path (SB 132, subject to approvals). Two offtake stacks can fail for **different** reasons.
3. **PPA prices are undisclosed.** Street can mark 658 MW × $7.2B backlog as if it were **~$115/MWh** (the S-1 illustration / Rystad range). If realized Cape prices are **below** that, the IPO book was using the high end of firm-power scarcity.
4. **DOE $45/MWh is a 2035 Shot, not Cape Phase I.** EIA AEO2026 **conventional** geothermal LCOE (with ITC, 2031 online) is **$40.38/MWh** in the page-8 stacked chart — **that is hydrothermal as modeled in NEMS, not EGS.** Using EIA hydrothermal to underwrite EGS LCOE is a category error.
5. **Services names do not get paid like IPPs.** Even if Cape works, HP/LBRT/NBR capture **dayrate/frac**, not 30-year PPA NPV.

**Notes that matter:** EIA says **direct LCOE comparisons across technologies are misleading**. Combustion-turbine LCOE is high because EIA spreads fixed costs over a **low capacity factor** (methodology PDF: peakers/storage use a **10%** CF in the LCOE formula). Geothermal ITC is **30% in 2025, 23% in 2026, 15% in 2027** then off, under OBBBA as EIA modeled it. **EGS is not a separate EIA technology row.**

Source: https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/aeo/electricity_generation/pdf/LCOE_report.pdf (fetched 2026-09-02).

Source: https://www.energy.gov/articles/doe-launches-new-energy-earthshot-slash-cost-geothermal-power

A back-of-envelope **not from the company**: at $7,000/kW, 83% CF, 30-year life, no fuel, the capital recovery alone is far above $45/MWh unless WACC and ITC do heavy lifting. That is why Fervo’s **disclosed** path is **$/kW down**, not a Cape LCOE of $45.

### 9.4 Nuclear restarts (PPA prices are estimates, not company prints)

- **Constellation (CEG) / Microsoft, Crane Clean Energy Center (TMI Unit 1 restart):** 20-year PPA announced **2024-09-20**. **Price not disclosed** by either company. **Bloomberg Intelligence estimate ≥ $100/MWh**; **Jefferies ~$112/MWh** (Bloomberg, 2024-09; Reuters, 2024-09-23). Target online **2028** (Constellation press).
- **Palisades (Holtec):** fuel load began **2026-08-30** (industry report). PPAs with Wolverine Energy cited; **$/MWh not in that announcement**.

**Read-through:** undisclosed **firm CFE** PPAs for nuclear restarts are being **marked by analysts in the same $100–115/MWh neighborhood** Fervo uses as an illustration. That is **consistent with scarcity pricing**, not evidence that EGS has cleared $45/MWh.

### 9.5 Trend

- EIA hydrothermal LCOE **with ITC** prints **cheap** (~$40/MWh in the 2031 chart) because NEMS geothermal is **not EGS** and still takes an ITC.
- EIA peaker LCOE **with low CF** prints **expensive** (~$173/MWh) — the right comparison for “firm on peak” is **LACE/value**, which EIA refuses to collapse into one number.
- **Contracted EGS PPA prices remain undisclosed.** The only dated **company** price-like objects are **$/kW** (7,000 → 5,500 → 3,000) and an **illustrative $115/MWh**.
- Watch: first **reported** Cape average realized $/MWh in FY2027 10-K/10-Q vs $115 illustration vs $45 Shot.
