Seven Years
at Half-Staff

Every day the flag comes down is a decision — five of them written into law each year, the rest ordered by the president after a death, a shooting, or a disaster. Across the last seven full calendar years the flag flew at half-staff on — days, or roughly — days a year. Below, each year is drawn as a barcode of 365 days.

    January 20, 2025. The one gap in the 30-day mourning period for Jimmy Carter: flags went to full staff for the inauguration, then back down for another eight days.
    May is the densest month. Peace Officers Memorial Day and Memorial Day are fixed — but Buffalo, Uvalde, San Jose and the one-millionth COVID death all landed in the same three weeks of May in different years.
    2024 is the outlier. Nineteen days, the lowest of the seven — and five of those were the standing observances that require no proclamation at all. Just four presidential orders fell in the whole year.

        What is counted. Calendar days on which the U.S. flag was ordered to half-staff nationwide, either by presidential proclamation or by the standing observances in the Flag Code (Peace Officers Memorial Day, Memorial Day until noon, the National Fallen Firefighters Memorial Service, Patriot Day and Pearl Harbor Remembrance Day). Overlapping orders count once. State-level orders by governors — far more numerous — are excluded.

        Sources and caveats. Compiled from presidential proclamations published in the Federal Register and contemporaneous White House and governors’ notices. Entries marked ~ in the tooltip have an approximate end date and should be checked against the Federal Register before republication.
