Method and corrections

The rules every graphic on this site is built under, what we currently know to be uncertain, and how to tell us we are wrong.

Open correction on the current graphic. Nine of the half-staff periods in Seven Years at Half-Staff have end dates we inferred from standard practice rather than confirmed against the proclamation itself. They are marked ~ in the tooltip and "approx": true in the data. Treat that graphic as a working draft until they are verified.

Sourcing

Every figure traces to a primary record. For US federal material that usually means the Federal Register, an agency's own bulk data, or the record of a proceeding. Where a contemporaneous news report is the only available confirmation of a date or a detail, it is used, but the entry is flagged as unconfirmed rather than quietly promoted to fact.

Counting

The counting rule is fixed before the chart exists, and it is written on the page. In general:

  • One unit counts once. If two orders cover the same calendar day, that day is one day, not two.
  • Overlaps resolve by specificity. A one-off event beats a standing annual observance for the same day, because the event is the thing being measured.
  • Scope is stated, not implied. "National orders only, state orders excluded" is a choice that changes the number by a lot, so it appears on the graphic.
  • Nothing is typed in twice. Totals, shares and rankings are computed from the dataset at render time, so a corrected entry propagates everywhere at once.

OmittedWe could not establish that the event met the graphic's definition. Notable omissions are named in the graphic's footnotes rather than left silent.

Corrections

Send the entry, what it should say, and the source that shows it — {{EMAIL}}. What happens next:

  • Factual errors are fixed in the dataset, which updates every derived number on the page.
  • The graphic's footer records what changed and when. Corrections are not silently absorbed.
  • If a correction changes the headline finding, it is stated at the top of the graphic, not in a footnote.
  • Reporters of substantive corrections are credited unless they ask not to be.

Limits worth knowing

Two things this site cannot do for you. It does not do statistical inference — these are counts of a record, and a count of a record is a count of what got recorded, which is not always the same as what happened. And it does not cover state and local records at anything like federal depth, because they are not published in comparable form.
