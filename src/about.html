About

Data to the People is an independent data-journalism project. It publishes a small number of carefully counted graphics about public life, built from primary records, free to read and free to reuse.

Why it exists

Most of what governments do is already public. It is published as PDFs of proclamations, as quarterly agency reports, as bulk downloads nobody opens. The record is open; the picture is not. The gap between "technically available" and "actually legible" is where this site works.

The other half of the reason is trust. A chart is a very efficient way to be confidently wrong, and most charts you see online cannot be checked — the number came from an article that came from a press release. So the standard here is that every figure is derived from a dataset shipped inside the page, and the counting rule is written down before the chart is drawn.

The long version of both is the site's first post: a statement of purpose.

What gets published

A graphic goes up when three things are true:

  • It comes from a primary record — a proclamation, a filing, a roll call, an agency dataset — not from someone else's summary of one.
  • The counting rule is explicit: what one unit is, how overlaps are handled, what is excluded and why.
  • The data ships with it, so a reader can find an error without asking permission.

What does not get published: charts assembled from other charts, single-source claims that cannot be re-derived, and anything where the interesting number turns out to rest on a definition we cannot defend.

How it is funded

Advertising, through Google AdSense. That is the whole model right now. It is disclosed here for a reason: ad-funded publishing creates a pull toward volume and toward whatever gets shared, and the counterweight has to be an editorial rule you can hold us to. Ours is the three-part test above — a graphic that fails it does not run, however well it would travel.

Advertisers have no involvement in what gets covered and see nothing before it is published. Ad slots are labelled and kept away from navigation and chart controls, so a misclick is never mistaken for engagement. See privacy and cookies for what advertising means for your data.

Reuse
