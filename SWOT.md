# SWOT — Data to the People

An honest read of an ad-funded independent data-journalism site, as it exists today:
one published graphic, no domain authority, no audience, no revenue.

Written August 2026.

---

## Strengths

**The method is the product.** Most data-viz sites publish a picture; this one publishes
the counting rule and the dataset alongside it. That is a real differentiator, it is hard
for a content farm to copy, and it maps directly onto what Google's quality guidelines
reward. It also gives you something to say that nobody else in the category is saying.

**Every figure is computed, not typed.** Totals, shares and rankings derive from a dataset
in the page. Correct one entry and the whole graphic updates. This removes the most common
failure mode in data journalism — the chart and the caption disagreeing after an edit —
and it makes corrections cheap enough that you will actually make them.

**CC BY 4.0 is a distribution engine.** Free commercial reuse with attribution means every
republication is an unsolicited backlink. Teachers, Wikipedia editors and newsletter
writers can use the work without asking, and the cost to you is zero.

**Technically clean.** No framework, one stylesheet, one deferred script, self-contained
pages, ad slots that reserve their space. The site will pass Core Web Vitals without
tuning, which is not true of most publishers.

**The first topic is well chosen.** "Why are flags at half-staff today" is a query that
spikes every time an order is issued — recurring, news-driven, permanently renewable
demand rather than a one-off.

---

## Weaknesses

**One graphic.** There is no site here yet in the sense Google or a reader means it. Ten
to twenty substantial pages is roughly the floor for both AdSense approval and for
ranking for anything.

**Nobody is on the masthead.** "Data to the People" is an entity with no human behind it.
For a publication whose entire pitch is trustworthiness, an anonymous byline is a
structural weakness — and it is the cheapest one on this list to fix.

**The flagship graphic is a working draft.** Nine of its half-staff periods have inferred
end dates. The whole proposition is accuracy; shipping the flagship with known-unverified
data is the sharpest internal contradiction in the project. Verify before promoting.

**Single-topic, single-author capacity.** Each graphic is days of records work. At that
rate, publishing cadence — the thing that compounds — is the binding constraint.

**No owned audience.** No newsletter, no list, no social presence. Every traffic spike
currently evaporates completely. Traffic you cannot recall is traffic you rent.

**One revenue line, and a thin one.** AdSense on a US general-interest site runs roughly
$3–$8 RPM. At 50,000 monthly pageviews that is $150–$400 a month. The model does not fund
the work at any realistic near-term traffic level; treat it as a floor, not a plan.

---

## Opportunities

**Own the federal-records niche.** "Data visualization" is saturated by Visual Capitalist,
Statista and The Pudding. "What the US federal record actually says, counted properly" is
not. Narrow is the advantage, not the limitation.

**Explainers around each graphic.** One deep interactive plus two or three short pages
answering the sub-questions triples the indexable surface for a fraction of the effort,
and solves the page-count problem honestly.

**Embeds.** A copy-paste iframe of a graphic, with attribution baked in, turns every
republisher into a permanent backlink and a recurring impression. This is how small data
shops punch above their traffic.

**Educational reuse.** CC BY plus a downloadable dataset makes these pages classroom
material. `.edu` links are the most durable authority available and cannot be bought.

**Better revenue than AdSense, later.** Once there is an audience: newsletter sponsorship
(RPM 10–50× display), commissioned graphics for newsrooms and NGOs, and reader support.
AdSense is the right first step because it needs no audience relationship — but it should
not be the last one.

**AI-search resilience through primary data.** Summaries can reproduce your headline
number. They cannot reproduce an interactive, a dataset or a method page. Lead with what
cannot be summarised.

---

## Threats

**Your audience blocks ads.** The people most likely to love a rigorous data site are the
most likely to run an ad blocker — plausibly 30–40% of this audience. The revenue model is
structurally mismatched to the readership it is designed to attract. This is the most
serious item on this page.

**AdSense approval is not guaranteed.** Google wants substantial original content, a
privacy policy, clear ownership and usually a few months of site history. A rejection is
recoverable but costs weeks.

**Google algorithm dependence.** A site with one traffic source is one core update away
from zero. Helpful-content and site-reputation updates specifically target sites that
look mass-produced; a low page count plus heavy templating can read that way to a
classifier even when the work is genuine.

**AI Overviews absorb the factual query.** "How many days was the flag at half-staff in
2024" is exactly the kind of question answered in the SERP now. Informational traffic to
pages like this is compressing across the board.

**An accuracy failure is existential here.** A site selling method rigour cannot survive a
visible, uncorrected error the way a general publisher can. The standard you set is the
standard you get judged against.

**Spiky, unrecallable traffic.** A Reddit hit gives 100,000 views in two days and nothing
afterwards. Without a capture mechanism, growth never compounds.

**Bigger players can enter cheaply.** Any established outlet can commission the same
graphic with more distribution behind it. Your defence is the method and the archive, not
the individual chart.

---

## What follows from this

The SWOT says the same thing four times: **the work is good and the business is fragile in
one specific way — you have no audience you own, and the one revenue line you do have is
the one your particular readers block.**

In priority order:

1. **Verify the nine flagged entries.** The flagship cannot be a draft. Nothing else on
   this list matters if the accuracy claim fails.
2. **Put a name and a face on it.** A real byline with verifiable prior work. Cheapest,
   highest-leverage change available.
3. **Get to ten to fifteen pages** using the explainer pattern, then apply to AdSense.
   Applying earlier risks a rejection you have to wait out.
4. **Add an email capture before you chase traffic.** Not after. Every spike before the
   list exists is spent permanently.
5. **Ship embeds with the second graphic.** Backlinks compound; ad impressions do not.
6. **Treat AdSense as validation, not income.** Its job is to prove there is an audience
   worth selling something better to — sponsorship, commissions, or reader support. Plan
   the second revenue line while the first is still small.
